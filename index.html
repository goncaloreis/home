The five lines, worked out

1↑

War of attrition

Staying beats being better.

Say one contender is twice as able but stops after three attempts, and another keeps going for thirty. The first gets there about half the time; the second, nineteen times in twenty.

2↑

Once and again

Every attempt moves the odds.

Few things work the first time. Say one attempt in ten works: seven attempts are better than even, and forty-four are all but certain. The arithmetic pays only whoever is still standing for the next one.

3↑

First principles

A copied attempt moves nothing.

That holds only if each attempt is new. Copies of a failed attempt fail the same way: the odds stay at one in ten, however many you make. So each attempt is worked out again, from what is known to be true.

4↑

No corners

A cut corner doubles the work.

It also holds only if each attempt is done properly. Cut enough corners to turn one in ten into one in twenty, and twenty-two attempts become forty-five.

5↑

The difficult

Hard ground empties first.

Most people try about five times. On easy ground that is enough for two in three, so it fills up. On difficult ground it is enough for fewer than one in four, and the rest stop. Whoever keeps going finds it nearly empty.
