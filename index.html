Given

  • Nothing is owed.

    Effort and talent give no claim on a result. Chance has a hand in every outcome and keeps no accounts.

  • No one knows enough.

    Knowledge is partial, scattered and provisional, ours included.

  • Time runs out.

    Every choice is paid for in it, and none of it comes back.

What follows

None of this is chosen. What we do about it is.

Each answer rests on two of the facts; the marks show which.

  • Read every result as evidence, never as a verdict.

    Nothing is owed, so no single result settles anything: chance had a hand in it. No one knows enough to say beforehand what will work, so every piece of work is a test. We look at each result for what it shows, good or bad, and do the work properly every time, because a careless test cannot be read at all.

  • Borrow no conclusions, and act before you are sure.

    No one knows enough, so an answer taken on authority is a guess in someone else’s name. Time runs out, so waiting to be sure is a decision too, and rarely the cheaper one. We take each question back to what can be checked, decide on that, and correct course as we learn.

  • Never stake the means to go again.

    Nothing is owed, so any one effort can fail however well it is made; a long run of them seldom fails entirely, and a long run is open only to those who last. Time runs out, so the efforts are numbered, and losing the means to continue loses every one that was left. We pace the work, hold something in reserve, and keep going.

We work where all three bite at once.

A problem is hard when its outcome is least certain, its answer least known and its road longest. Those are the problems most people leave early, which is why they stay open, and they are where the answers above count for most.

The call

One call, on one problem that will not move. We take it back to the facts, work out what follows, and tell you what we would do and why. The call is paid.
