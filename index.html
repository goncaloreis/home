The argument, line by line

1The world is a war of attrition.

Most things worth having go to whoever is still there when the others have stopped. Everything below follows from this.

2The job is to get up, once and again.

Few things work the first time. Say one attempt in ten works. Then one attempt is a long shot, seven are better than even and forty-four are all but certain. The arithmetic only pays whoever is still standing for the next attempt.

3To think from first principles.

The arithmetic assumes each attempt is new. Copy someone else and you fail the way they did; repeat yourself and you fail the way you did last time. So every attempt is worked out again, from what is known to be true.

4To cut no corners.

It also assumes each attempt is done properly. Cut enough corners to turn one in ten into one in twenty, and forty-four attempts become ninety.

5To go for the difficult.

Easy ground is crowded; anyone can try there. Difficult ground is where a war of attrition can be won, because it is where the others stop first.
