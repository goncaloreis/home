Given

  • Every result is partly luck.

    Effort and talent shift the odds; they do not decide the outcome.

  • No one knows enough.

    Knowledge is partial, scattered and provisional, ours included.

  • Time runs out.

    Every choice is paid for in it, and none of it comes back.

What follows

Facts set no aim. Ours is to build businesses that last.

Held to that aim, each pair of facts rules out a great deal. Each answer below is what one pair leaves, and its mark shows which.

  • Trust no single result.

    Every result is partly luck, so a single one rarely settles anything. And since no one knows enough to say beforehand what will work, every piece of work is a test. We run each one cleanly, because a careless test teaches nothing either way, and we change the approach when the evidence says so.

  • Check what you can, and act on the rest.

    No one knows enough, so no answer is safe on authority alone: where it can be checked, we check it. Where it cannot, we act anyway, because time runs out and waiting for certainty usually costs more than acting without it.

  • Keep every loss survivable.

    Any one effort can fail however well it is made, because every result is partly luck; what counts is how many good efforts you get to make. Time runs out, so those are numbered, and losing the means to continue loses every one that was left. We size each commitment so that losing it leaves enough for the next.

We work where all three bite at once.

A problem is hard when its outcome is least certain, its answer least known and its road longest. There the answers above count for most.

The call

One call, on one problem that will not move, in a business you are building or backing. We take it back to its facts, work out what follows, and tell you what we would do and why. The call is paid; you will know the fee before you book.
